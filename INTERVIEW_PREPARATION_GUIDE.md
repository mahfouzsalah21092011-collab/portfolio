### Question 2: What is your exact role and methodology in reviewing tenant MEP drawings?
**Objective:** Show technical rigor without overstepping into unauthorized design stamping or unverified load calculations.

**Model Answer:**
> *"My role is consultant-side technical review and coordination. When a commercial tenant submits their fit-out design, I evaluate their shop drawings against five core criteria:*
> 1. *Base-Building Capacity Alignment: I verify that the tenant's requested electrical load, cooling tonnage, and domestic water consumption stay strictly within the developer's allocated feeder and riser capacities.*
> 2. *Service Corridors & Containment: I check cable tray routing, chilled water pipe pathways, and ductwork elevations to prevent clashes within ceiling voids and ensure clear service corridors.*
> 3. *Plumbing & Drainage Inverts: I examine drainage tie-in levels and ensure appropriate gravitational slope gradients, making sure grease traps are incorporated for food and beverage tenants before discharging into the mall collection system.*
> 4. *Fire & Life Safety Integration: I verify that sprinkler layouts meet hazard classifications, fire alarm loops interface seamlessly with the mall's central fire alarm control panel, and smoke management adheres to Civil Defense requirements.*
> 5. *Maintainability: I ensure access panels and isolation valves are specified so future maintenance can be performed without disrupting adjacent tenants.*
>
> *I consolidate these observations into structured technical review comments, coordinate directly with the tenant's engineering consultant to resolve discrepancies, and ensure an approved set of drawings is in place before site work commences."*

### Question 3: How do you coordinate drawing reviews with site execution?
**Objective:** Prove that you don't just sit in the office—you bridge technical drawings with physical site reality.

**Model Answer:**
> *"Reviewing drawings is only half the battle; the real value is ensuring that what was approved on paper is what actually gets built in the unit. My coordination process follows a disciplined four-stage sequence:*
> 1. *Pre-Execution Site Walk: Before the tenant contractor begins MEP works, I conduct an initial site survey with their site engineer to verify base-building stub-out locations, isolator points, and invert levels.*
> 2. *First-Fix Containment & Rough-In Inspection: I inspect physical conduit runs, cable trays, pipe pressure-test manifolds, and duct hanging brackets before any ceiling closure or screeding. If a contractor reroutes a pipe or changes a cable size on site without authorization, I flag the non-conformance immediately.*
> 3. *Testing & Pre-Commissioning Checks: I witness hydrostatic pressure tests on water and firefighting lines, megger testing on power cables, and duct leakage inspections to ensure safety before live energization.*
> 4. *Snagging & Final Handover: Once fit-out execution is complete, I conduct a detailed technical snagging review, documenting any open items and verifying fire alarm loop testing with central BMS/fire panels prior to granting operational clearance.*
>
> *This continuous loop between reviewed drawings and physical field inspections prevents defects from being concealed behind walls or ceilings."*

### Question 6: How do you manage multiple tenant submissions and competing execution priorities during peak fit-out phases?
**Objective:** Highlight project management foundations, organizational discipline, and clear communication under pressure.

**Model Answer:**
> *"During peak mall fit-out phases, you might receive dozens of drawing revisions while multiple contractors demand urgent site inspections. To prevent bottlenecks, I apply three core principles:*
> 1. *Centralized Review Tracking Register: I track every submission in an organized register logging receipt date, review turnaround, status (Approved / Revise & Resubmit / Rejected), and critical technical comments. This provides full visibility to project developers and tenant coordinators.*
> 2. *Triage by Critical Path: I prioritize drawing reviews and site inspections based on tenant opening schedules and utility milestones. Anchor tenants, banking institutions, and complex F&B units with heavy ventilation and grease-drainage requirements are prioritized because their lead times directly impact common-area systems.*
> 3. *Standardized Review Checklists: By using structured technical checklists covering electrical loads, HVAC connections, plumbing levels, and Civil Defense requirements, I maintain high review quality and consistent turnaround times without compromising engineering rigor.*
> 4. *Batch Site Inspections: Rather than ad-hoc site visits, I schedule coordinated morning and afternoon inspection rounds, allowing tenant contractors to group their milestone checks and keeping execution moving forward."*
